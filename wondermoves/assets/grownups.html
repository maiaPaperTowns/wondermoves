How each game works (and why)

Every game uses one movement, counts a movement when it is completed (never how fast), and stays within the range you approve. Evidence levels describe the therapy approach each game is built on, not WonderMoves itself.

💰 Treasure Reach

One movement: reach forward from the hand rest, then come back.
  1. Rest the forearm on the towel, hand facing the Distance sensor.
  2. Reach toward the sensor until the meter fills, and hold for a moment.
  3. Come back to rest. Each reach clears a layer; 4 reveal the treasure.
Approach: goal-directed training. Evidence: moderate
Hand function +3.8 AHA, goal achievement +2.3 COPM vs control (Burgess et al. 2025, network meta-analysis of 48 RCTs). Rated effective in Novak et al. 2020.

🦴 Fossil Brush

One movement: one back-and-forth brushing stroke.
  1. Sit facing the camera with the brushing hand in view.
  2. Watch Pip's stroke first, then copy it: swing one way, then back.
  3. Each completed stroke clears the same amount of sand, slow or fast.
Approach: action observation (watch, then do). Evidence: emerging
Hand function +4.9 AHA in unilateral CP (Burgess et al. 2025) but no self-care effect, and a 2023 meta-analysis found a smaller, uncertain effect. UP-CAT trial (Sgandurra et al. 2013) improved daily arm use.

🫧 Bubble Pop

One movement: lift the hand up from rest.
  1. Rest both hands on the table, in view of the camera.
  2. Lift a hand to pop a bubble, then rest.
  3. Pairs: lift both hands together (take as long as you need).
Approach: bimanual training (both hands). Evidence: strong
The most consistently effective approach: hand function +4.6 AHA, goals +3.3 COPM, self-care SMD 0.39 (Burgess et al. 2025). Rated effective in Novak et al. 2020.

Levels: strong = effective for hand function, goals and self-care; moderate = effective for hand function and goals; emerging = effective for hand function only, with mixed results across reviews. Full sources: docs/evidence-brief.html.
