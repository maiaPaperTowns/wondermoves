# WonderMoves: 3-minute demo video

About 340 words of voiceover, spoken calmly, like you're showing a friend. That leaves room for the game sounds and pauses while the shots play. **[Shot]** is what's on screen; the indented text is the voiceover. Numbers stay in the opening. Each scene is its own clip, so you can trim in editing.

---

## 1. Hook (0:00–0:15)

**[Shot]** Close-up: a hand slowly reaching for a cup. Cut to the WonderMoves logo.

> In the US, one in 345 kids has cerebral palsy. For many, one hand just won't do what they ask, and the way to get it moving is practice, lots of it, at home. WonderMoves turns that practice into games.

## 2. The kit (0:15–0:30)

**[Shot]** Slow pan over the table: the Arduino UNO Q, the distance sensor on its mount, the webcam, a laptop showing the game.

> Here's the whole kit: an Arduino UNO Q with a Qualcomm chip, a small distance sensor, and a webcam. Everything runs on the board itself, so no video ever leaves the house.

## 3. Treasure Reach (0:30–1:10)

**[Shot]** The app opens straight into Treasure Reach. Zoom in on the cue card above the chest: "Touch the sensor!" with the little finger sliding to the sensor.

> The app opens right into our first game, Treasure Reach. The card tells the child exactly what to do: touch the sensor.

**[Shot]** Split screen: a hand slides across the table to the sensor; on screen the card changes to "Keep going!" then "Almost! Touch it!", and the bar fills.

> As the hand slides closer, the bar fills and the card cheers them on. It only asks for the distance a parent or therapist approved, never more.

**[Shot]** The finger rests on the sensor: "Hold it there…", the star fills, the chest pops open, a treasure appears.

> Hold it for a moment, and the chest pops open. Slow counts just as much as fast.

**[Shot]** A shorter reach that doesn't make it: "⭐ Great try!" floats up and Pip says something kind.

> And if a reach falls short, they still get a star for trying, because effort matters.

## 4. Made for every child (1:10–1:30)

**[Shot]** Menu → Play with → 🔘 Button. A hand presses the big "Hold to reach" button; the chest opens. Then Settings → Comfort: tick Calm mode; back in the game, the clouds and confetti are gone.

> Not every child can use a camera or a mouse, so you can also play with one big button or an accessibility switch. And Calm mode turns off flashing and busy effects, and softens the sounds, for kids who are sensitive to them.

## 5. Fossil Brush (1:30–1:50)

**[Shot]** Pip draws a zigzag; then a hand in front of the webcam traces it in the air, and sand brushes away to show a fossil.

> In Fossil Brush, Pip shows a movement first, then the child copies it to brush sand off a fossil. Watch, then do.

## 6. Bubble Pop (1:50–2:05)

**[Shot]** A hand moves onto rising bubbles; they pop. Tap "I'm done".

> In Bubble Pop, you move your hand onto floating bubbles. There's no timer: the child decides when they're done.

## 7. For grown-ups (2:05–2:40)

**[Shot]** Tap "Grown-ups". The PIN screen; type the PIN.

> Grown-ups have their own space, behind a PIN.

**[Shot]** The Progress page: the child's card, then scroll to "Reach progress": reaches completed, average reach length, the daily trend line.

> Parents see how much their child practised, how far the reaches went, and how that changes day by day. No scores and no comparisons with other kids.

**[Shot]** A session card with its Databricks note.

> After each session, Databricks writes a short, plain note about what happened.

**[Shot]** Settings: hand to practise, approved reach, break reminder, and "About your child" with the nickname and favourite things.

> And this is where a parent sets the hand to practise, the safe reach, and what Pip should know about their child, all kept on the board.

## 8. Close (2:40–3:00)

**[Shot]** Home screen: "Hi, Mia! Pick a game." Montage of the three games, then the logo.

> It already works with real hands. Next, we want to run a home pilot with an occupational therapist. We're WonderMoves, and every move counts.

## Before filming

- **Board:** plugged in, running the latest version, distance sensor on its mount with a mark for the hand about 15 cm away.
- **Parent PIN:** create it before filming, and use a PIN you're happy to show on screen (or blur it).
- **Clean data:** play a few real sessions first so the Progress page has real reaches and a trend; don't show test data.
- **Databricks note (scene 7):** only film it after a real Databricks run worked on the laptop helper. If it isn't working on the day, cut that shot.
- **Child profile:** use a made-up nickname such as "Mia". Don't film a real child without a parent's written consent; a teammate's hand works.
- **Settings to reset after filming:** turn Calm mode back off, and switch Treasure Reach back from Button to Sensor.
- **Captions:** add them. The video is about accessibility.
