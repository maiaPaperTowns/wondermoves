# WonderMoves: 3-minute demo video script

About 400 words of voiceover, at a calm speaking pace. Times are approximate. **[Shot]** is what's on screen; the indented text is the voiceover.

---

## 0:00–0:20 Hook

**[Shot]** Close-up of a child-sized hand (or a teammate's hand) reaching for a toy, slowly. Then cut to the WonderMoves title screen with Pip.

> About 1 in 345 children has cerebral palsy. Many have one weaker hand, and that hand gets better through practice: the same movement, hundreds of times, at home. WonderMoves turns that practice into games.

## 0:20–0:45 The problem

**[Shot]** On-screen text, one line at a time: "> 25 hours of practice = biggest gains", "Fewer than half of families keep it up", "Timers + speed = wrong for CP".

> A 2025 meta-analysis of 48 trials found that children who got more than 25 hours of practice gained the most. But home practice is hard to keep up, and most motion games run on timers and speed. That's the opposite of what children with CP need.

## 0:45–1:05 The kit

**[Shot]** Slow pan over the hardware on a table: Arduino UNO Q, Modulino Distance, Movement and Thermo sensors, Logitech Brio webcam. Label each one briefly.

> Everything runs on an Arduino UNO Q with a Qualcomm chip. The sensors and webcam watch the movement, and the pose AI runs on the board itself, offline, so no video ever leaves the house.

## 1:05–1:35 Treasure Reach

**[Shot]** Split screen: a hand reaching toward the distance sensor, and the chest opening on screen. Show one slow reach that still counts.

> Treasure Reach practises one movement: reaching forward. The distance sensor sees the hand arrive, the child holds it for a moment, and the chest opens. A slow reach counts just like a fast one. There's no timer, and the target never goes past the range the therapist set.

## 1:35–2:00 Fossil Brush

**[Shot]** Pip demonstrates a stroke, then the child's hand copies it in front of the camera, and the fossil appears.

> In Fossil Brush, Pip shows the stroke first, then the child brushes. Every completed stroke uncovers more of the fossil.

## 2:00–2:25 Bubble Pop

**[Shot]** One hand lifts and a bubble pops, then both hands lift together for a "pair" bubble.

> Bubble Pop is about lifting: the weaker hand, the other hand, then both together. Two-handed practice has the strongest evidence, so the game builds it in, and it always starts with the hand the grown-up chose.

## 2:25–2:45 For grown-ups

**[Shot]** The Grown-ups page: affected-hand setting, break reminder, Today's practice counts. Then the parent website.

> Parents and therapists set the limits, choose the affected hand, and see how much practice happened today. After a while the game suggests a break, but it never cuts the child off.

## 2:45–3:00 Close

**[Shot]** Montage of the three games, then the logo with the line "Every move counts."

> It already works with real hands: 23 bubble lifts in two minutes, seven of them with both hands. Next, we want to run a home pilot with an occupational therapist. We're WonderMoves. Every move counts.

---

### Filming notes

- Record each game segment as its own clip, so the timing can be trimmed in editing.
- Keep the board's screen recording and the hand footage in sync: either a split screen or a picture-in-picture.
- Don't film a real child without parental consent. A teammate's hand works for the demo.
- Treasure Reach needs the board reconnected and the distance sensor working. Do a test reach before filming.
- Add captions; the video is about accessibility.
